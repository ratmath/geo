Conic sections

Give conicProperties any equation \(Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\) and it says what the curve is and finds everything a course asks about it: the centre or vertex, the axes, vertices, foci, eccentricity, directrices and asymptotes. The answers are exact, square roots and all, even when the conic is turned by an angle that is not a nice one.

What kind of conic

Two numbers decide it. The determinant \(\Delta\) of the symmetric matrix \(\begin{pmatrix} A & B/2 & D/2 \\ B/2 & C & E/2 \\ D/2 & E/2 & F \end{pmatrix}\) says whether the conic is degenerate, and the discriminant \(B^2 - 4AC\) says which shape it has:

\(B^2 - 4AC < 0\)\(= 0\)\(> 0\)
\(\Delta \ne 0\)ellipse (circle when \(A = C\), \(B = 0\)), or empty when \((A + C)\Delta > 0\)parabolahyperbola
\(\Delta = 0\)a single pointtwo parallel lines, a double line, or emptytwo crossing lines

A degenerate or empty conic returns its type and nothing more.

Centre and axes

An ellipse or hyperbola has a centre, where the gradient of the left side vanishes: a 2 × 2 linear system. Moving the centre to the origin leaves \(\lambda_1X^2 + \lambda_2Y^2 + F' = 0\) in the directions of the eigenvectors of \(\begin{pmatrix} A & B/2 \\ B/2 & C\end{pmatrix}\), with \(F' = \Delta/(AC - B^2/4)\). Then \(a^2 = -F'/\lambda\) along the axis whose \(\lambda\) is smaller in size (for a hyperbola, the one whose \(\lambda\) has the sign of \(-F'\)), and \(c^2 = a^2 \mp b^2\). With \(B = 0\) the axes are the coordinate directions and everything is rational or one square root; with \(B \ne 0\) the eigenvalues are square roots and the directions are algebraic numbers, still exact.

const e = conicProperties("9x^2 + 4y^2 - 36 = 0");
e.type;          // "ellipse"
e.a; e.b;        // 3, 2
e.foci;          // [(0, √5), (0, −√5)]
e.eccentricity;  // √5/3

const h = conicProperties("x^2/16 - y^2/9 = 1");
h.asymptotes;    // ["3·x − 4·y = 0", "3·x + 4·y = 0"]
h.directrices;   // ["5·x − 16 = 0", "5·x + 16 = 0"]: x = ±a²/c

conicProperties("x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3").axes.major;   // x + y = 0, with a² = 6, b² = 2

Parabolas

When \(B^2 = 4AC\) the quadratic part is a perfect square and one eigenvalue is 0; its eigenvector is the direction of the axis. In coordinates along and across the axis the equation becomes \((w - w_0)^2 = 4p(s - s_0)\): the vertex is \((s_0, w_0)\), the focus is \(p\) along the axis from it, and the directrix is \(p\) the other way.

const p = conicProperties("x^2 - 4y - 2x + 9 = 0");
p.vertex;      // (1, 2)
p.focus;       // (1, 3)
p.directrix;   // y − 1 = 0
conicProperties("x^2 - 2xy + y^2 - 4x - 4y = 0").focus;   // (1/2, 1/2): a parabola turned 45°

A projective Conic (see the projective plane) has the same answers through conic.properties(), and circleFromEquation is the circle case.
